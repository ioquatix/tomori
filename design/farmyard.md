# The Farmyard

This describes the farmyard environment, `farmyard`, in crates of its own, `tomori-environment-farmyard` and `tomori-environment-farmyard-client`: an evening yard with a chicken coop, which participants walk about by teleporting. It follows the common and client convention of `environments.md`, is built as another project would build an environment, from Tomori's published crates and their extension points, and is the proof that a downstream environment crate can customise everything about its environment without changing Tomori.

## Requirement

**Offered by Tomori's own programs.** Tomori's own server program, `tomori-server` in `tomori-server-app`, offers the farmyard, labelled Farmyard, after Sky and the grid, at the identity in its common crate, `tomori_environment_farmyard::ID`, `VERSION` (1) and `LABEL`; and Tomori's own app, the headset, desktop window and console, and its spectator present it, so an owner can create a space in it, or change a space to it, from the Space section of the hand menu (see `environments.md`). Each registers it explicitly, as it registers Sky: the server with `Builder::environment`, the app with `tomori::Builder::register(tomori_environment_farmyard_client::register)` and the spectator by calling it on its sceneries. The core's builders start empty, as for every environment, so a build of another project's offers or presents the farmyard only if it registers it the same way.

**Its own crates.** Everything about the farmyard is in its own crates: `tomori-environment-farmyard`, its common crate, what its server and its clients share, building no renderer or GPU stack: its identity, its layout, its coop and yard models, built in code as `tomori-assets` models with that crate's GPU-free core, the chicken's asset, its animator graph and its `ChickenController`, the chickens' behaviours, the flock, the encoding of its state and intents, and its server side, `FarmyardSide`; and `tomori-environment-farmyard-client`, its client half: its scenery, drawn with `tomori-assets`' renderer, the hens skinned on the GPU, its teleport profile, and its client side, which plays the chickens' clucks. It has no server crate: what `environments.md` puts in one is what must never reach a client, such as credentials, data sources and the options that configure them, and the farmyard has none. Its server side is the flock's simulation, which every client replays with the same functions (`Behaviour::place`, the controller and the animator), the validation of its intents, and their limits, none of which is secret, and it builds only on `tomori-environment`'s interface, not on `tomori-server`; so it is kept beside what it shares, and a server crate of its own would hold no more than its registration. Should the farmyard gain something server-only, it moves into `tomori-environment-farmyard-server`. Tomori's own crates provide only the extension points it builds on: `tomori-assets`' models, renderer and animator (see `assets.md` and `animation.md`), `tomori-render`'s `Scenery` and `Sceneries`, `tomori-scene`'s locomotion profiles and client sides, and the builders' registration. Nothing in Tomori's core crates knows about chickens or coops, as the dependency rule in `bake/tests/dependencies.rs` checks, naming the farmyard's crates among those it must find; where the farmyard needs a hook that Tomori lacks, the hook is added in general, for any environment, and its pull request says so.

**The yard.** In the space's scene coordinates, with home, where participants arrive, at the origin facing −Z: a fence of weathered posts and rails around a yard 10 m wide and 12.6 m deep, with a gate in front of home between two taller posts, one with a lantern; packed earth inside, worn along a path to the coop, and meadow beyond to the horizon, with pines around. In the yard: the coop at the back left; a water trough on the right; a round feeder with grain scattered about it in the middle; and hay bales stacked in the back right corner, with loose straw. `layout.rs` holds where each is, which the scenery, the teleport profile and the chickens share.

**The coop.** A red hen house with white trim, raised on four legs, its floor 0.6 m up, under a pitched roof that overhangs every side, with its little door facing home at the top of a cleated ramp, the door swung open beside it, a lantern on a bracket by the door, and a window in its side towards the middle of the yard, through which participants peek in at the straw, two nest boxes along the back and a perch, in the shade of a dark lining. It is a model of its own, in its own space, built in code (`coop.rs`) and placed on the layout's footprint; a coop modelled in Blender replaces it through the same loader as every asset, `Coop::Asset`, with the built one standing in if it cannot be loaded (see the crate's `context/coop.md`).

**The look.** Low-poly, built in code, like Sky's shrine: every face flat, its colour varied a little face by face. A moonlit evening: a deep blue sky with a dusky band at the horizon and the last warmth of sunset low in the west, stars overhead and a moon rising in the east; the moon's cool light and the sky's and ground's ambient light on everything, and the lanterns' warm light falling off within a few metres, their glass glowing; haze over the far meadow. The scenery grows out of a space through the reveal sphere, as every scenery does.

**Walking the yard.** The farmyard declares a teleport profile, beside its scenery, as `locomotion.md` describes: participants land on the yard's packed earth, clear of the coop, its ramp, the trough, the feeder and the hay, on four rectangles at the ground's height, which include home and the spot beside the coop's window to peek in from, and turn in snaps of 30°. It is the first of Tomori's own environments to allow travel.

**The chickens.** A flock of three to five hens wanders the yard, the chickens Hinoki Maple Williams modelled and animated, each the same model, one wearing a traffic cone. They walk, a step with each foot, as far as their feet carry them; peck at the ground; look about; now and then one goes up the ramp, in at the door and to a nest or the perch to roost, and comes out again a while later; and they keep clear of everything in the yard and of each other's paths. A participant who points at one confuses it: it turns to them, tilts its head with a questioning cluck and holds it while they point, and straightens it when they look away. One pointed at for 4 seconds, or by two participants at once, is startled: it squawks, spins round and sprints a metre and a quarter away, the clearest way it can, at most once a minute. A chicken on the ramp, in the coop or running cannot be pointed at. Now and then a chicken clucks of its own accord. Each has a name, and the agent is told, when a participant points at one as they talk, who she is, whether she wears the cone, what she is doing and where, as `Ada: [Pointing at Pip, wearing a traffic cone, a hen in the farmyard, pecking at the ground by the feeder] what's she eating?`.

**Model, view and controller.** The chickens follow `animation.md`'s split:

- **The model** is the chicken's asset, `chicken.glb`, and what each chicken does, which the farmyard's server side owns. A chicken's state is a `Behaviour`: when it began, on the farmyard's clock, where the chicken was and which way it faced, and what it does: stand, peck until a moment, look to a side until a moment, walk to a point, be confused by someone at a place, recover, sprint a way, or roost. Where that puts the chicken at any later moment is computed by one function, `Behaviour::place`, from the walk's and sprint's root motion, on the server to plan and on every client to draw, so that the server never streams where a chicken is.
- **The view** is an instance of the chicken's model, its cone shown or hidden, drawn with the yard and the coop by the farmyard's scenery, every chicken in one draw for each of its two meshes.
- **The controller**, `ChickenController`, is the farmyard's own, and deliberately specific: it sets the chicken's animator's parameters, `walking`, `confused` and `startle`, at the moments each behaviour says, including when a walk turns and stops, places the instance where the behaviour puts it, leaning as it pecks, and hears the animator's events, the cluck as it tilts its head and the squawk as it sprints. The animator's graph is the farmyard's own too: standing, the walk looping at the chicken's own pace, the tilt, a held tilt that sways, straightening, and the sprint, cross-fading between them.

**The server side.** The farmyard's server side, `FarmyardSide`, in its common crate, which Tomori's own server registers with the farmyard through `Environment::with_server_side`, runs the flock of each space bound to the farmyard, from the space's identifier, so that a space's flock has the same chickens, names and paces each time it starts. Its clock is the time since it started, in milliseconds. Every 50 ms, or as an intent arrives, it moves the flock on: each chicken whose behaviour has ended begins its next at the moment the last ended, so that it stops and starts where its feet are down. It shares the flock's state, with its clock, for whoever joins, and announces each change as an event with the state it leaves; and it describes every chicken to the agent once a second. The chicken is loaded from the program's assets, once, as clients load it, so that both agree on how far it goes; a server that cannot load it shows the side's error in the space.

**Intents.** A participant's client asks the side to `notice` a chicken, from where they stand, as they begin pointing at it, and says they look `away` as they stop. The side validates each against the flock: a chicken it has, on the ground and not running; a place that is a number, taken as at most 50 m away; at most once in 6 seconds from each participant for each chicken, and once in 1.5 seconds from each for any, so that sweeping the ray across the flock does not set them all clucking; and looking away only from a chicken they point at. A chicken's reaction begins 150 ms after the server takes it, so that every client has it before it shows. A chicken nobody has said they looked away from recovers after 8 seconds of its own accord.

**The clock.** Each message says the farmyard's clock as it was sent, so a client knows it to within the time a message takes to arrive: a state sets it, and an event that arrives later than the clock says moves it on. Clucks of the chickens' own accord are drawn from the space, the chicken and each 30 seconds of the clock, the same for everyone; a client hears only the clucks and reactions of the last second, so that joining, or a clock that jumps, clucks nothing.

**The client side.** The farmyard's client side is a `ClientSide`, registered with its scenery: it keeps a `ChickenController` for each chicken the server side shares, moves them on by the clock, picks the chicken the ray meets first, by a sphere about its body, named by her name, asks the side to notice it and looks away again, sends the clucks and squawks its controllers hear as ambient sounds from each chicken's head, and gives its scenery the chickens to draw.

**The chicken's asset.** `crates/environment-farmyard/assets/models/chicken.glb`, named in code by its digest, embedded everywhere but on Android, where the APK carries it as `assets/farmyard/chicken.glb`, is exported from its author's three `.blend`s, kept in `assets/source`, by the export script there, with Blender in background mode and `--disable-autoexec`; the export is deterministic. Its feet are its last leg bones, and each foot's sole is the lowest point of the mesh that moves with that foot; the walk's root motion counts as planted the feet within 2% of the chicken's height of the lowest, which carries it about 8 cm a loop of a second and a half, rocking back a little as each foot comes down, and the sprint's counts every foot, as it scrambles, which carries it about 1.24 m. The chicken stands on its feet, 0.4 m tall at the top of its walk, cone and all. It is by Hinoki Maple Williams, and is licensed, with its `.blend` sources, under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence (CC BY 4.0), as `assets/models/LICENSE.md` says, which credits her, links the licence and says that the export changed nothing but the format. That notice is the credit the licence asks for wherever the chicken goes: the farmyard's client crate adds it to a client's sceneries as it registers, `tomori --licenses` shows it, and the APK carries it as `assets/farmyard/LICENSE.md`. The crate as a whole is `MIT AND CC-BY-4.0`.

**What is the farmyard's and what is Tomori's.** The farmyard needed these general hooks, which any environment can use: the environment state channel (`environments.md`); `tomori-assets`' loading, model instances, skinned drawing and animator (`assets.md`, `animation.md`); a client side, `ClientSide`, registered with `Sceneries::set_client_side`, driven by the frame step, picking what the ray meets as `Resource::object` and drawing through `Scenery::animate`; ambient sounds, `Feedback::ambient`, played by the mixer at a place in the scene (`feedback.md`, `spatial-audio.md`); licence notices, `Sceneries::add_license`, which `tomori --licenses` shows; and Tomori's own APK's `assets` folder, `android/assets`, read by an `ApkAssets` source. Everything about chickens, flocks, coops and clucks, their state's encoding and their intents, is in the farmyard's crates, and nothing in Tomori's core crates names them, but for the link to its models in Tomori's own APK's `android/assets`, which the app's packaging carries as it registers the farmyard; a downstream environment could do the same without changing Tomori.

## Rationale

In November 2026 the maintainer asked for a farmyard environment to give the chickens that their daughter modelled, animated and agreed to make public, an official home, and to use it to exercise and grow the environment framework: a scenery built procedurally in low-poly code to match Sky's shrine, with a fenced yard of grass and packed earth, a coop with a ramp and a door that is a separate model their daughter can later replace with her own, a little hay, a water trough and a feed area, and a cosy, consistent palette, a moonlit or warm evening. The maintainer then set the principle that the point of a dedicated environment is the ability to customise everything about it, so everything of the farmyard's is in its own crates, built on general extension points, and Tomori's own programs register it as another project's would. Once the core crates started empty (#200, #201), the farmyard was reworked onto the convention they set: its identity moved from `tomori-protocol` into its common crate, and its registration from the core's default builders into Tomori's own programs. The coop's door faces home, so that arriving participants see it and the chickens' comings and goings; its window faces the yard's middle, so that peeking in needs no climbing. Lamps light through walls, as nothing casts shadows yet, so the inside of the coop is lined dark to stay in the shade.

The chickens were first an animated scene in Sky, baked at build time; the maintainer asked for them to move to a farmyard of their own, their behaviour owned by the server and their motion by a general, deterministic animator, so that everyone in a space sees the same chickens doing the same thing, and so that the farmyard exercises everything an environment needs. The server sends what a chicken does and since when, rather than where it is each frame, because the animator and root motion compute the rest the same everywhere, and because a chicken's behaviour changes a few times a minute while it moves every frame. Startling is rare, held for 4 seconds or by two at once and once a minute, so that pointing at a chicken to talk about it mostly gets a puzzled look, and the limits on notices keep a sweep of the ray from being a flood. Clucks of their own accord are drawn from the clock rather than sent, as they change nothing. The walk's stride differs from the first bake's, 8.3 rather than 6.8 cm a loop, because each foot's sole, rather than its bone, is what is planted now; it is to be checked on the headset for sliding.

## Scope

`crates/environment-farmyard`: its identity in `lib.rs`, `layout.rs`, `shapes.rs`, `props.rs`, `coop.rs`, `chicken.rs`, `behaviour.rs`, `controller.rs`, `cluck.rs`, `flock.rs`, `side.rs` and `assets/`; `crates/environment-farmyard-client`: `scenery.rs`, `sky.wgsl`, `client.rs` and its registration in `lib.rs`; its registration in `tomori-server-app`'s `builder`, with its server side, `tomori-client-quest`'s `app` and `tomori-client-spectator`'s `app`; and the APK's `android/assets/farmyard` link to its models.

## Validation

- Unit tests check the layout: everything inside the fence, every walkable rectangle clear of everything that stands in the yard and inside the fence, home and the spot beside the coop's window walkable, and the coop's door and ramp where the layout says.
- Unit tests check that the yard and the coop are checked models, that the coop fits its footprint and its ramp reaches the ground at its foot, that a coop from an asset replaces the built one, and that the built one stands in for one that cannot be loaded.
- A rendering test on lavapipe draws the farmyard from home, checking an evening sky darker overhead than the ground, and a red coop, and from beside the coop's window, and growing out of a point; and the sky's shader validates. Set `TOMORI_FARMYARD_PREVIEW` to a PNG path to save both views.
- Tests check that the farmyard registers its scenery at its identity and its teleport profile, that Tomori's own app and spectator list it in `Hello` and that the app declares travel only for it, that Tomori's own server offers it after Sky and the grid, with its server side, and, in `tomori-server-app`, that the server and Tomori's own app and spectator agree on every one of Tomori's environments, the farmyard included.
- The dependency rule's test finds the farmyard's common and client crates, and fails if the common crate builds the renderer or wgpu, or if a core crate depends on either other than through its `app` feature.
- Tests check the chicken against Blender's own deformation, every seventh vertex of both meshes at several frames of each animation, within a millimetre of Blender's units; that it stands on the origin at its height; its stride and sprint; its animator's walk, tilt and sprint, with their steps, cluck and squawk; and that a model lacking any part it needs is refused.
- Tests check behaviours: turning the short way, a walk that turns, goes as far as whole steps carry it and stops with its feet down, quicker at a quicker pace, and climbs a ramp; pecking, looking and confusion keeping it where it is; and a sprint spinning round and running its distance.
- Tests run flocks of several spaces for twenty minutes each and check that the same space gives the same flock, that chickens keep in the yard and clear of what stands there and of each other, walk and roost; that a notice confuses a chicken until its participant looks away, within the limits, and that one held long enough or by two startles it, at most once a minute; that a chicken on the ramp, in the coop or running cannot be noticed; and how each is described.
- Tests run the server side with a space of their own and check that it shares the flock, describes it, answers a notice with an event and the state it leaves, passes over what it cannot read, and stops with its space, or with an error its space refuses or without its chicken.
- Tests of the controller and the client side check that a chicken wears its cone only if it has one, steps where its behaviour puts it and clucks and squawks as it should; that the client side shows nothing before a state, reads the clock from messages, picks the nearest chicken by name, asks for and ends notices within their limits, hears reactions and clucks of their own accord, the same for two clients, and nothing late; and, on lavapipe, that the scenery draws the chickens it is given. `TOMORI_FARMYARD_PREVIEW` saves them too.
- On the headset: change a space to the farmyard, confirm that it is cosy and legible at night, that teleporting about the yard lands on the earth and never on the coop, the trough or the hay, and that peeking in at the coop's window shows its nest boxes. Watch the chickens walk without their feet sliding, point at one to confuse it and hold to startle it, hear their clucks from where they are, and ask the agent about one.
