# The Farmyard

This describes the farmyard environment, `farmyard`, in crates of its own, `tomori-environment-farmyard` and `tomori-environment-farmyard-client`: an evening yard with a chicken coop, which participants walk about by teleporting. It follows the common and client convention of `environments.md`, is built as another project would build an environment, from Tomori's published crates and their extension points, and is the proof that a downstream environment crate can customise everything about its environment without changing Tomori.

## Requirement

**Offered by Tomori's own programs.** Tomori's own server program, `tomori-server` in `tomori-server-app`, offers the farmyard, labelled Farmyard, after Sky and the grid, at the identity in its common crate, `tomori_environment_farmyard::ID`, `VERSION` (1) and `LABEL`; and Tomori's own app, the headset, desktop window and console, and its spectator present it, so an owner can create a space in it, or change a space to it, from the Space section of the hand menu (see `environments.md`). Each registers it explicitly, as it registers Sky: the server with `Builder::environment`, the app with `tomori::Builder::register(tomori_environment_farmyard_client::register)` and the spectator by calling it on its sceneries. The core's builders start empty, as for every environment, so a build of another project's offers or presents the farmyard only if it registers it the same way.

**Its own crates.** Everything about the farmyard is in its own crates: `tomori-environment-farmyard`, its common crate, what its server and its clients share, building no renderer or GPU stack: its identity, its layout, and its coop and yard models, built in code as `tomori-assets` models with that crate's GPU-free core; and `tomori-environment-farmyard-client`, its client half: its scenery, drawn with `tomori-assets`' renderer, and its teleport profile. It has no server crate, as nothing of it runs only in the server. Tomori's own crates provide only the extension points it builds on: `tomori-assets`' models and renderer (see `assets.md`), `tomori-render`'s `Scenery` and `Sceneries`, `tomori-scene`'s locomotion profiles, and the builders' registration. Nothing in Tomori's core crates knows about chickens or coops, as the dependency rule in `bake/tests/dependencies.rs` checks, naming the farmyard's crates among those it must find; where the farmyard needs a hook that Tomori lacks, the hook is added in general, for any environment, and its pull request says so.

**The yard.** In the space's scene coordinates, with home, where participants arrive, at the origin facing −Z: a fence of weathered posts and rails around a yard 10 m wide and 12.6 m deep, with a gate in front of home between two taller posts, one with a lantern; packed earth inside, worn along a path to the coop, and meadow beyond to the horizon, with pines around. In the yard: the coop at the back left; a water trough on the right; a round feeder with grain scattered about it in the middle; and hay bales stacked in the back right corner, with loose straw. `layout.rs` holds where each is, which the scenery, the teleport profile and the chickens share.

**The coop.** A red hen house with white trim, raised on four legs, its floor 0.6 m up, under a pitched roof that overhangs every side, with its little door facing home at the top of a cleated ramp, the door swung open beside it, a lantern on a bracket by the door, and a window in its side towards the middle of the yard, through which participants peek in at the straw, two nest boxes along the back and a perch, in the shade of a dark lining. It is a model of its own, in its own space, built in code (`coop.rs`) and placed on the layout's footprint; a coop modelled in Blender replaces it through the same loader as every asset, `Coop::Asset`, with the built one standing in if it cannot be loaded (see the crate's `context/coop.md`).

**The look.** Low-poly, built in code, like Sky's shrine: every face flat, its colour varied a little face by face. A moonlit evening: a deep blue sky with a dusky band at the horizon and the last warmth of sunset low in the west, stars overhead and a moon rising in the east; the moon's cool light and the sky's and ground's ambient light on everything, and the lanterns' warm light falling off within a few metres, their glass glowing; haze over the far meadow. The scenery grows out of a space through the reveal sphere, as every scenery does.

**Walking the yard.** The farmyard declares a teleport profile, beside its scenery, as `locomotion.md` describes: participants land on the yard's packed earth, clear of the coop, its ramp, the trough, the feeder and the hay, on four rectangles at the ground's height, which include home and the spot beside the coop's window to peek in from, and turn in snaps of 30°. It is the first of Tomori's own environments to allow travel.

## Rationale

In November 2026 the maintainer asked for a farmyard environment to give the chickens that their daughter modelled, animated and agreed to make public, an official home, and to use it to exercise and grow the environment framework: a scenery built procedurally in low-poly code to match Sky's shrine, with a fenced yard of grass and packed earth, a coop with a ramp and a door that is a separate model their daughter can later replace with her own, a little hay, a water trough and a feed area, and a cosy, consistent palette, a moonlit or warm evening. The maintainer then set the principle that the point of a dedicated environment is the ability to customise everything about it, so everything of the farmyard's is in its own crates, built on general extension points, and Tomori's own programs register it as another project's would. Once the core crates started empty (#200, #201), the farmyard was reworked onto the convention they set: its identity moved from `tomori-protocol` into its common crate, and its registration from the core's default builders into Tomori's own programs. The coop's door faces home, so that arriving participants see it and the chickens' comings and goings; its window faces the yard's middle, so that peeking in needs no climbing. Lamps light through walls, as nothing casts shadows yet, so the inside of the coop is lined dark to stay in the shade.

## Scope

`crates/environment-farmyard`: its identity in `lib.rs`, `layout.rs`, `shapes.rs`, `props.rs` and `coop.rs`; `crates/environment-farmyard-client`: `scenery.rs`, `sky.wgsl` and its registration in `lib.rs`; and its registration in `tomori-server-app`'s `builder`, `tomori-client-quest`'s `app` and `tomori-client-spectator`'s `app`.

## Validation

- Unit tests check the layout: everything inside the fence, every walkable rectangle clear of everything that stands in the yard and inside the fence, home and the spot beside the coop's window walkable, and the coop's door and ramp where the layout says.
- Unit tests check that the yard and the coop are checked models, that the coop fits its footprint and its ramp reaches the ground at its foot, that a coop from an asset replaces the built one, and that the built one stands in for one that cannot be loaded.
- A rendering test on lavapipe draws the farmyard from home, checking an evening sky darker overhead than the ground, and a red coop, and from beside the coop's window, and growing out of a point; and the sky's shader validates. Set `TOMORI_FARMYARD_PREVIEW` to a PNG path to save both views.
- Tests check that the farmyard registers its scenery at its identity and its teleport profile, that Tomori's own app and spectator list it in `Hello` and that the app declares travel only for it, that Tomori's own server offers it after Sky and the grid, and, in `tomori-server-app`, that the server and Tomori's own app and spectator agree on every one of Tomori's environments, the farmyard included.
- The dependency rule's test finds the farmyard's common and client crates, and fails if the common crate builds the renderer or wgpu, or if a core crate depends on either other than through its `app` feature.
- On the headset: change a space to the farmyard, confirm that it is cosy and legible at night, that teleporting about the yard lands on the earth and never on the coop, the trough or the hay, and that peeking in at the coop's window shows its nest boxes.
