# Locomotion

This describes how a participant travels in a space beyond the room they stand in, and how an environment allows it. It is the first part of the locomotion in #78.

## Requirement

**Environments declare it; Tomori owns the devices.** An environment declares a locomotion profile, and Tomori's clients turn the controllers, the keyboard and the mouse into travel for it. There are three:

- **None**, the default: room scale, as before. Nothing in this document changes anything in a space whose environment declares none, or anywhere outside a space. Sky and the Minimal grid declare none; the farmyard declares teleport about its yard (see `farmyard.md`).
- **Teleport**: point and release onto walkable surfaces that the environment declares, with a preview, and turn in snaps of 30°.
- **Fly**: teleport, and rise as well, up to a ceiling the environment declares; landing on a surface brings the participant back down to it.

There is no smooth artificial walking or turning in any profile; the only continuous artificial movement is rising, where an environment declares fly.

**Declared with the scenery, for now.** Until environments have server sides (#75), a headset build declares an environment's profile beside its scenery, by the environment's identifier, through `tomori::Builder::locomotion` or `tomori-render`'s `Sceneries::set_locomotion`, as `builders.md` describes. Both are provisional: once an environment's server side exists (#180), the profile is to move there, and these may be deprecated. A client applies the profile only inside a space bound to that environment, and only when it draws the environment's scenery: a client that shows the grid for an environment it lacks applies none, since the surfaces would not be seen. Once the server side exists, it is to declare the profile, so that everyone in the space has the same one.

**Walkable surfaces.** A walkable surface is a horizontal rectangle, aligned with the scene's axes, or a disc, at a height, in the space's scene coordinates. The environment declares only where a participant may land, already clear of edges and obstacles. A teleport lands where the pointing ray first meets a declared surface from above, if that is at least 0.25 m along the ray and at most 15 m from the ray's origin horizontally; the ray must point at least about 1° below the horizontal; and a surface hides any beneath it from a ray that passes through it first, even where it is too near to land on. Nothing else blocks the ray in this first pass, since environments declare no colliders yet. A ray that meets no surface by these rules has nowhere to land.

**An interface that can grow.** `tomori-scene`'s `Locomotion` and `Surface`, and each of their variants with fields, are `#[non_exhaustive]`, made with their constructors, `Locomotion::teleport` and `Locomotion::fly`, `Surface::rectangle` and `Surface::disc`, so that colliders (#97), a fly speed, rotated surfaces or a form declared by the server can be added without breaking a build that declares them, as `clients.md` asks of `tomori-client` and `spaces.md` of `Resource`.

**The rig.** Everyone in a space is in one shared space, the space's scene coordinates, and each headset tracks its participant relative to their own play space, their room. Travel is a per-participant rig, a translation and a turn about the vertical, local to each client, that takes the participant's room into the shared space. Each client applies its participant's rig to the head, eyes, controllers, hands and pointing ray before the scene, pointing, the panels held by the hands, rendering, presence and the listener see them, so that all of them agree on where the participant is. A teleport moves the rig so that the head stands above the landing at the same physical height, with the room's floor at the surface's height; a turn turns the rig 30° about the head, which stays where it is; flying raises the rig at 0.6 m/s, never above the ceiling. Travel belongs to one space in one environment: the rig goes back home, where it starts and where the participant stood before, on leaving the space, on joining another, when the space's environment changes, and whenever the profile is none, before that frame's poses are placed with it, or, for a change the frame makes itself, such as leaving from the hand menu, from the next frame. A teleport, turn or rise applies from the next frame, so that every pose in a frame is placed with the same rig. Recentring is unchanged: it places the room again as before and keeps the rig. The status pill stands in the participant's room (see `interaction.md`), on the headset and in the desktop window alike, so travelling carries it along.

**Controls.** On the headset, in a space whose profile is teleport or fly, the left stick, which does nothing else in a space (see `interaction.md`):

- **forward**, while the right controller points, aims a teleport, and returning the stick to the middle lands there. While it aims, the ray ends at the landing, highlighted, with a ring on the surface, or is plain and short where there is nowhere to land, and returning the stick then does nothing. Losing focus, the right controller's ray or the space, or the hand menu or the right wrist's transcript showing, cancels the aim without travelling, and so does a return in the frame the ray is lost, or the left controller's stick being lost, which reads as returned.
- **left or right** turns 30° that way, once per push.
- **back**, held, rises, only where the profile is fly.

Each push of the stick is one gesture, owned by the direction it first passes its threshold in, more forward than sideways or more sideways than forward or back: forward at 0.7 from the middle, sideways at 0.6, back at 0.7. The gesture lasts until the stick returns within 0.2 of the middle, and nothing else starts meanwhile: easing off while aiming, as from 0.9 to 0.55, keeps aiming rather than landing, and rolling the thumb sideways or back while aiming neither lands nor turns; a push that turned aims nothing, and turns no more, however it rolls; rising continues while the stick is still held back past 0.2, and rolling it elsewhere stops rising without turning or aiming. A push forward without the ray, or one that is cancelled, or that began while travel was inactive, as before the scenery grew in, does nothing until the stick returns to the middle. While a teleport is aimed, the ray serves only it: no worker or card is pointed at, and the right grip picks up nothing. While a card is carried (see `github.md`), the left stick does nothing, since aiming would put the card down. Nothing else changes meaning, and travel never happens while the hand menu or the right wrist's transcript shows, nor before the space's scenery has grown in on entering it, or on a change of environment, so that its surfaces are seen. Hand tracking alone does not travel in this first pass, since it has no stick to aim with.

**The desktop window** does the same with the left stick's keys, the arrow keys (see `desktop.md`): hold **↑**, point the mouse at a surface, and release **↑** to land at its ring; **←** and **→** turn; and **↓**, held, rises. A released key returns its stick to the middle. The window draws the ring but no ray, as its cursor shows where it points.

**Feedback.** The ray finding a landing while a teleport is aimed ticks as hover does, once until it has nowhere to land again, and landing clicks as choosing does, felt on the right controller (see `feedback.md`). Turning and rising have no cue: the view moving is their signal.

**A blink on landing.** A teleport does not cut from one place to the next: the frame in which it lands is drawn dark, from where the participant was, and the view comes back from dark over 0.2 s from where they landed, on the headset and in the desktop window alike, panels included. Turns and rising do not blink. The spectator, whose audience is not the one travelling, does not blink.

**Turning travel off.** In a space whose environment allows travel, the hand menu's Space section offers **Travel: on**, under "On this headset", beside the status pill, and choosing it turns travel off on this headset, **Travel: off, room scale**, and on again. Off, the left stick travels nowhere and the rig goes home, as for a profile of none, so the participant stays room scale; nothing else changes. It is kept on the headset, as the status pill is, for whichever space they are in.

**Presence carries where each person travelled.** Presence carries the head, controllers and hands in the shared space (see `spaces.md`), so a client sends them with its rig applied, without a change to the protocol. To the server and every other client, a participant is only those positions: whatever shows presence shows each person where they travelled, today the spectator, which follows a participant's head and draws their controllers and hands (see `spectator.md`), and each voice, which comes from its head (see `spatial-audio.md`); participants' own clients draw no other participants yet. The rig itself is not sent, since nothing needs it: it may be revisited if something such as drawing a participant's play-area boundary does. The preview, the ray and the ring, is drawn only for the participant aiming.

**Voices are where people are.** Spatial audio places the listener and every source in the shared space: the listener at their head where they travelled, and each other participant at their head from presence, where they travelled, so that two people who travelled to meet hear each other where they stand. Two people can be in the same place, as when both stand at the centre of their own play space at home; that is allowed, and they can move apart (see `spatial-audio.md`).

**Known limits.** The spectator draws the sceneries it is given (see `builders.md`): `tomori`'s spectator modes draw the build's, but `tomori-spectator` draws only Tomori's own, so it shows another build's environment, the kind that declares travel, as the grid, from where the participant travelled. Comfort, the blink, the snap angle, the stick's thresholds and the ring's size are still to be confirmed on the headset.

## Rationale

Issue #78 and the environment framework proposal (`context/environments.md` on the `environment-framework-proposal` branch, section 5) set locomotion profiles, declared by the environment, with `none` the default, `teleport` onto declared walkable surfaces with a preview and 30° snap turns, and `fly` for vertical travel; no smooth artificial motion by default; travel as a per-participant rig of translation and yaw; presence carrying it, so that others and the spectator see each person where they travelled; and recentring unchanged. Teleport and snap turns avoid the discomfort of smooth artificial movement. The maintainer's prototype Fantail Observe proved the rig, teleports that keep physical eye height, snap turns about the head, a ceiling for flying and landing on the nearest supported floor, which this follows.

The issue and the proposal leave the controls and the details of each profile open; the first pass, in November 2026, took the details below from Fantail, where it proved them, or chose them where Tomori differs, and its pull request lists the choices for the maintainer's review. The left stick carries all of travel, rather than Fantail's right stick for turning and left stick click for flying, because Tomori's left stick is free in a space while the right stick moves the right wrist's focus and pages result cards and the left stick click mutes spoken replies; the maintainer confirmed this in November 2026, reviewing the first pass, so that turning never moves the wrist's focus. Fly includes teleport, as in Fantail, since rising without a way down is no use. Profiles are declared beside the scenery until the server side that the proposal gives them exists. Presence already carries poses in the space's scene, so sending them with the rig applied carries travel without a protocol change. The reach of 15 m is measured horizontally, unlike Fantail's along the ray, so that a participant who has flown high can still land straight below. A release in the frame the ray is lost cancels, as in Fantail, since a controller that stops being tracked reads as a released stick; a card carried blocks travel, since the board puts down a card whenever the ray is taken from it.

The review of the first pass found that a stick eased from 0.9 to 0.55 landed, and that rolling the thumb from forward to (0.8, 0.5) landed and turned in one frame, because aiming ended as soon as the stick was no longer pushed forward. A thumb resting near the threshold, or drifting sideways while aiming, landed the participant somewhere they did not choose, which is the discomfort teleport is meant to avoid. Landing only as the stick returns to the middle gives the aim hysteresis, as #78 asks for pinch, and giving each push to the direction that engaged first keeps a turn and a landing from sharing one gesture. Forward needs a firmer push than sideways, since aiming takes the ray from everything else, while a turn is over at once. The blink and the setting to turn travel off came from the same review's comfort findings: an instant cut is jarring, and #78's rationale for teleport is comfort, so anyone who still finds travel uncomfortable can stay room scale. Rising has no vignette, and the snap angle no setting, yet.

The maintainer decided, reviewing the first pass in November 2026, that the builder's declaration stays, documented as provisional, rather than waiting for the environment's server side (#180). The first pass kept voices on seats around the listener, carried by the listener's rig, and left carrying the others' rigs in presence to #183; the maintainer then decided, in November 2026, that travel stays local to each client and presence carries only shared-space positions, with every voice at its head and no seats, since that already places everyone where they are (see `spatial-audio.md`).

## Open questions

- How profiles move to the environment's server side (#75, #180), so that everyone in a space has the same one, and how a client that cannot draw the environment then behaves.
- The snap angle, the thresholds and the blink's length, once tried on a Quest; whether turns should blink too, whether rising needs a vignette, and whether the snap angle should be a setting.
- Whether hand tracking gets a teleport gesture, and whether surfaces gain colliders that block the ray (#97).

## Scope

`crates/scene`'s `locomotion.rs` (the profiles, surfaces, landing, the rig, the stick's gestures and the blink), the frame step's use of it in `scene.rs`, `scene/headset.rs` and `scene/desktop.rs`, the conversation's profile for its space and the setting that turns it off in `conversation.rs` and `hand_menu.rs`, and the voices at the heads in the shared space in `spatial_audio.rs`; `crates/render`'s `Sceneries`, and the ring and the blink drawn by its renderer; `crates/client-quest`'s builder and the clients that hand the profiles to the conversation and the listener to spatial audio (`lib.rs`, `xr.rs`, `crates/client-desktop`'s `desktop_participant.rs`, and `crates/client-spectator`).

## Validation

- Unit tests of the rig: a teleport keeps the head's physical height above the landing, on surfaces at different heights; a turn keeps the head where it is and turns the rig by 30°; rising stops at the ceiling, and landing comes back down.
- Unit tests of landing: the nearest surface the ray meets from above, rectangles and discs, a higher surface hiding a lower one, even one too near to land on, nothing for a ray pointing level or up, nothing nearer than 0.25 m or further than 15 m horizontally, and nothing for a profile of none.
- Unit tests of the controls: forward aims and returning to the middle lands, once; returning with nowhere to land, after losing the ray, in the frame the ray is lost, or while inactive, does nothing; left and right turn once per push; back rises only for fly; leaving the space, joining another in the same environment, changing environment and a profile of none send the rig home.
- Unit tests of the gestures: easing from 0.9 to 0.55, 0.3 and 0.21 keeps aiming, and only the middle lands; rolling from forward to (0.8, 0.5), to either side or back neither lands nor turns, and the return lands once without turning; pushes short of each threshold start nothing; a turn's push, rolled forward, aims nothing; part of the way back is not the middle; a push forward without the ray, or one cancelled, does nothing until the middle; a stick reading nonsense while aiming lands nothing; rising continues while held back and stops, without turning or aiming, as the stick rolls away.
- Unit tests of the blink: landing darkens the view fully, and it comes back over 0.2 s, while travel is inactive too; turning does not darken it. A rendering test on lavapipe that the blink darkens the whole view, background and all, to black when full, partly when half, and not at all when open or given nonsense, with and without multisampling.
- Tests of the setting: the Space section offers it beneath the status pill only where the environment allows travel, saying whether it is on; choosing it turns the profile to none and back.
- Spatial audio tests: a participant who teleported onto a ledge and turned is heard where their presence puts them; and when two participants have travelled, one standing 1 m in front of the other, the listener hears them 1 m in front.
- Tests that the presence the headset's frame and the desktop window send has the rig applied: another participant in the space receives the head where the participant travelled.
- A desktop window test, on lavapipe, that after travelling and with the transcript showing, the window draws exactly what the renderer draws with the space and its panels both taken to the participant's room, and that drawing either from home instead differs.
- Frame step tests: in a space with a teleport profile, the ray ends at the landing with its ring, workers are not pointed at while aiming, a carried card is not put down by the stick, the hover and choose cues are given, the landing frame is dark and drawn from where the participant was, and the next frame's head, controllers, a tracked hand, listener and presence are where the participant travelled, as are the headset's eye matrices and the desktop window's view, while the pill stays with the participant; with a profile of none, or outside a space, the left stick changes nothing.
- Documentation tests that the constructors build a profile and that a variant's fields cannot be given directly outside `tomori-scene`.
- A test that the conversation applies a declared profile only in a space bound to an environment it draws, and that Tomori's own build declares a profile for the farmyard alone.
- On the headset: confirm that teleporting and snap turns are comfortable, that the ring is easy to see and place, that the stick's thresholds suit a real Quest, and that the spectator sees and hears from where the participant travelled.
