# Spatial Audio

This describes where a client's playback sounds from, how its sources are balanced, and the first step of echo cancellation for the live microphone. What is played is described in `live-audio.md` and `speech.md`, and the sound cues in `feedback.md`.

## Requirement

**Each client places every source in its own scene.** The server sends one mono stream per source, unchanged; each client renders them in stereo for its own listener, so that the mix follows the head with no network delay.

| Source | Where it sounds from |
| --- | --- |
| Agent speech, live or played again | the middle of the swarm, as it moves |
| Another participant | their head, from their presence |
| A sound cue | towards the hand that acted, from the agent for a request or a catch-up, or in the middle (see `feedback.md`) |
| A source the client cannot place | in the middle of the head, at unity, as before |

**One shared space, and every voice at its head.** Everyone in a space is placed in one shared Euclidean space, the space's scene coordinates. Each headset tracks its participant relative to their own play space, and travel (see `locomotion.md`) is local to each client: its rig, a translation and a turn about the vertical, maps that play space into the shared space. The server and every other client see a participant only as positions in the shared space: presence carries their head, controllers and hands there, already with their rig applied (see `spaces.md`). Every other participant's voice therefore comes from their head as their latest presence gives it, with nothing moved, seated or corrected; one whose head is not known yet, or is not finite, plays in the middle of the head, at unity. There are no seats. Two participants can be in the same place, such as both standing at the centre of their own play space, and that is allowed: they can move apart, and the panner keeps a voice in the same place as the listener from being deafening or flipping from ear to ear (see **Near field**, below). Spreading people out as they arrive, if it is wanted, belongs to environments, with spawn or arrival anchors (#79), not to audio.

**The listener.** The headset listens from its head each frame and the desktop window from its participant's head (see `desktop.md`), in the shared space, after its own rig, so where they have travelled to. The spectator, which renders from the head of the participant it follows, hears as that participant does: from their head as their presence gives it, in the shared space, with the others at their heads as their presence gives them and the followed participant's own voice in the middle; without one to follow, it hears from its neutral view (see `spectator.md`). The console has no listener, so it hears every source and cue in the middle.

**Panning, not head-related transfer functions.** For each source, relative to the listener's head:

- **Direction:** equal-power panning by how far the source is to the side, scaled so that a source straight ahead is at unity in each ear, as mono playback was, and so that the far ear is never more than 10 dB below the near one, since the Quest's speakers are open to both ears.
- **Interaural delay:** the far ear is delayed by up to 0.6 ms, as a fractional delay.
- **Head shadow:** a one-pole low-pass on the far ear, more for a source to the side and most for one behind and to the side, where it reaches 2.5 kHz, and a gentler one on both ears for a source behind, so that front and back differ.
- **Distance:** unity at 2 m, growing no louder within 0.5 m, at most 12 dB above, and never more than 12 dB quieter for a person or 6 dB for the agent, so that distance changes presence but never intelligibility.
- **Near field:** within 0.5 m, the direction, the panning, the far ear's delay and the head shadow, blends linearly towards the middle of the head, reaching it at the listener's own head, at equal power throughout, so that a voice passing through or standing in the same place as the listener neither flips from ear to ear nor from front to back. This is a rendering detail only: where each source is does not change.
- **Smoothing:** each source's gains, delays and filters follow where it is about two thirds of the way in 30 ms, gliding within each audio callback, so that turning the head is followed closely and a voice that jumps, as when someone teleports, or a change to mono, sweeps without a click and with little change of pitch.

**Mono audio.** The Sound section's **Mono audio**, kept with the Feedback setting on the device (see `feedback.md`), and the console's `feedback mono on|off`, play every source and cue in both ears alike, for people who hear with one ear; distance still changes loudness and ducking still applies. An output with one channel plays mono in the same way.

**Ducking.** Agent speech, live and played again, eases down by 8 dB while any participant speaks: once a participant's level in each mix, measured without the speaking indicator's slower release, has been at least −34 dBFS for 100 ms, counting through gaps of up to 60 ms between syllables, it goes down over 80 ms, stays down through pauses of up to 250 ms, and comes back up over 500 ms. A click or cough shorter than 100 ms does not duck it. A participant whose live microphone picks up the agent from their speakers, without echo cancellation, sends it back as their own voice, which can duck the agent for everyone else; measuring echo (below) shows how much. Ducking is local and is not stopping: the stream keeps playing and being consumed, and the speaking indicator still measures it before its gain (see `live-audio.md`). Sound cues are never ducked and duck nothing.

**Ambient sounds.** An environment's ambient sounds (see `feedback.md`) are placed where they are in the scene, as a person's voice is in direction and distance, but without the interaural delay or head shadow, which would need a filter for each; they are mono samples at their own rate, which the mixer plays at its own, in a straight line between samples. At most three play at once, a new one replacing the one furthest through, and one never begins while anyone can be heard speaking: agent speech playing and not muted here, or a person loud enough to duck it.

**The mixing order.** Each source's jitter buffer; its level, measured for the speaking indicator; its local gain (the agent's mute) and ducking; its placement in stereo; the ambient sounds, placed; the sound cues; push-to-talk's silencing fade; the limiter.

**Real time.** The application gives the player the listener and the sources' positions once a frame, writing the whole scene each time. The audio callback takes only the latest, without waiting and without allocating for it, by swapping it with the one it held; a scene being written is taken at the next callback. The cues are synthesized when the speaker opens, before it starts, and the echo probe only for the echo test; the callback synthesizes them only if the device's rate changes.

**Outputs.** The headset opens AAudio with two channels, the desktop writes left and right to its device's first two channels and silence to any others, and a device with one channel plays mono.

**Echo cancellation, first step.** While the live microphone is on, the headset:

- captures with Android's voice-communication input preset in an audio session it allocates;
- asks for the platform's acoustic echo canceller on that session, enabling it if it is not, and logs whether one was created and whether it is enabled;
- plays as voice communication in the same session, reopening the speaker when the live microphone turns on, and as media again when it turns off, keeping what it has buffered so that a reply is not cut short; if the device opens the two at different rates, the player starts again at the new rate, and the speaker's log of each opening shows its rate. The live microphone captures in the same session each time it opens, so push-to-talk during live audio, which reopens it, does not reopen the speaker. If the speaker cannot open as voice communication, it plays as media and logs why.

At startup it logs whether the device reports an echo canceller, a noise suppressor and automatic gain control. Each time the live microphone opens, it logs whether the noise suppressor and automatic gain control are on in its session, if it has one, the audio mode, and the call and media volumes, since voice communication plays at the call volume and media at the media volume; at `info` the first time in a session, and at `debug` when it opens in the same session again, as after push-to-talk. If the measurements show no cancellation, putting the device in communication mode while live is the next thing to try.

**Measuring the echo.** Started with the intent extra `tomori.echo_test on`, the headset measures each time the live microphone opens, timed from when the speaker has opened as voice communication in the microphone's session; a speaker that fell back to media does not count, and after 5 seconds without one the test logs that it measured nothing. It listens to the room from 0.3 to 1 second, then plays a speech-band probe at −20 dBFS RMS through its own player for 1.5 seconds, rising and falling about four times a second as syllables do, so that a noise suppressor does not take it for steady noise, and listens from 0.3 to 1.3 seconds into it. It logs how the speaker played, the level heard during the probe, how far that is below the probe and above the room, saying when it is within 3 dB of the room and so only an upper bound, and the room's level. The canceller hears nothing from the speaker until the probe starts, so the measurement may catch it still adapting, which understates what it removes once settled; and a noise suppressor may still take some of the probe for noise. With `tomori.echo_test canceller-off`, everything is the same but the echo canceller is disabled, so the difference from `on`, at the same call volume, is what the canceller removes; this is the comparison that isolates it. With `tomori.echo_test uncancelled`, the live microphone captures with the recognition preset, as push-to-talk does, and the speaker plays as media, so the difference from `on` is what the communication path as a whole removes, including any noise suppression, and depends on the media volume against the call volume, which are on different scales; both are logged. The test needs a quiet room, no one speaking, and a person to run it; push-to-talk during it silences the probe.

**What stays until echo is measured.** The feedback warning in `live-audio.md` stays until measurements on the device justify dropping it; no bar for dropping it is set yet. Push-to-talk keeps silencing playback; it is to duck other sound instead only once echo cancellation is verified.

## Rationale

In November 2026 the user, speaking from the headset, asked for the audio to be designed, with the agent's voice coming from the swarm and participants' voices from their avatars; until then every client played one mono mix that sounded as if it came from the middle of the head, so the agent could not be told from a person by direction and several speakers masked each other. The audio and haptics proposal (`docs/proposals/audio-and-haptics.md`) recommended placing sources on the client, with panning rather than head-related transfer functions, which costs a few operations per sample on the Quest. The user accepted its first stage and decided that echo is to be measured first, the feedback warning staying until measurements justify dropping it; and that push-to-talk is to duck rather than silence other sound only once echo cancellation is verified. Unity at 2 m, rather than the proposal's 1 m, keeps the agent about as loud as the mono mix was. The first stage seated remote participants on a circle of 2.5 m around the listener, moved by their heads within 1 m, so that people at their own homes did not sound inside each other; with travel (#181), that placed someone who had travelled to meet the listener on a seat rather than where they stood, and carrying each participant's rig in presence to correct it (#183) grew complicated. The maintainer decided in November 2026 on the model above instead: one shared space, travel local to each client, every voice at its head from presence, overlap allowed and handled by the panner's near field, and no seats anywhere; the rig is not sent, since presence's poses are already in the shared space, though it may be revisited if something such as drawing a participant's play-area boundary needs it. A nearest distance of 0.5 m, rather than the first stage's 1 m, lets someone standing close sound close, while 12 dB above the reference is not deafening. Allocating one audio session for the live microphone and playing as voice communication in it is how Android expects its echo canceller to be used; switching only while the live microphone is on keeps media playback, and its volume, unchanged otherwise.

## Scope

`crates/client`'s player and mixer (`playback/spatial.rs`, `playback/duck.rs`, `playback/mixer.rs`, `playback/player.rs`), its outputs (`speaker.rs`), the live microphone's session (`capture.rs`) and the echo test (`connection.rs`), in `crates/scene` the scene of voices (`spatial_audio.rs`) and the rendering of the player for a device (`playback.rs`), and in `crates/client-quest` the listener in each frame loop, the desktop's devices (`desktop_audio.rs`, `cpal_audio.rs`), and the headset's devices and echo canceller (`android_audio.rs`, `android.rs`).

## Validation

- Unit tests cover the panner: a source on the left is louder and earlier in the left ear, with the far ear shadowed; the far ear never falls below 10 dB under the near one and power is equal in every direction; a source ahead at the reference distance is at unity; distance is clamped near, at 0.5 m, and far, with the agent's floor above a person's; nearer than 0.5 m the direction blends towards the middle, so that a source 1 cm to either side, or just ahead or behind, sounds almost alike, at equal power; turning the head moves the sound; a source behind is duller; mono pans nothing; rendering delays the far ear by the interaural delay; and a source jumping from left to right glides without a step.
- Unit tests cover the scene of voices: each participant is placed at their head from presence, one in the same place as the listener too, and one whose head is unknown or not finite is not placed; one who travelled is heard where their presence puts them; and when two participants have travelled, one standing 1 m in front of the other, the listener hears them 1 m in front, and to the side after turning. Connection tests cover reading back what was placed.
- Unit tests cover ducking: it waits for the onset, counting syllables through short gaps, goes down over the attack and back over the release after the hold; and in the mixer, agent speech is ducked under a person speaking while its level is still measured, and a single loud frame does not duck it.
- Unit tests cover the mixer and player in stereo: without a scene both ears play the mono mix, and without a listener cues are centred; sources and agent speech played again are placed on their side; mono plays the same in both ears; cues sound towards their side or the agent; silencing fades both ears together; the latest scene is the one rendered, and one set after a render is written whole; and outputs with one, two or four channels are filled correctly.
- Unit tests cover the echo test's windows and its log line, its waiting for the speaker to play in the microphone's session and giving up after 5 seconds when it plays as media, the probe's level and its rise and fall, and the probe being loaded only for the test. Connection tests cover rerouting the speaker only when the route changes, reopening an open one once.
- On the headset: confirm that the agent sounds as if it comes from the swarm, that a second participant sounds as if they stand where they are, and that standing in the same place as them is neither deafening nor jumping from ear to ear, and that turning the head moves the sound without clicks. Confirm that the agent ducks while a person speaks. Check logcat for the audio effects line at startup, and for the live microphone's session, the echo canceller's state and the speaker playing as voice communication when the live microphone turns on. Check that the speaker still opens with two channels as voice communication, and that the volume buttons change its volume while live. Run the echo test with `tomori.echo_test on` and `canceller-off` at the same logged call volume, and with `uncancelled` noting the media volume, and record the differences, the volumes and the device in `live-audio.md` once they justify changing the warning. Check that the speaker opens at the same rate as media and as voice communication.
- Unit tests check that an ambient sound plays once at the mixer's rate, resampled, and ends; that a fourth replaces the one furthest through; that it never begins over a person speaking or agent speech playing, but does over agent speech muted here; and that one to the left is louder in the left ear.
